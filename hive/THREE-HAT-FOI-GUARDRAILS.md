# Three-Hat and FOI Correspondence Guardrails

Version: 1.0.0
Effective: 24 November 2026
Authority: Hive JOIN contract v3.31.0
Scope: every recipient-facing email draft, reply, follow-up, FOI/EIR/SAR request, internal review, complaint, preservation enquiry and every HubSpot redraft or material edit.

This file defines the mandatory method. It contains generic operating rules only. Recipient-specific correspondence, private chronology, contact details, case identifiers, recipient-specific commitments and draft text remain in the private HubSpot task and source mailbox. This file is not a recipient profile and must not be used to publish case material.

## 1. Three mandatory hats — one actual reviewer

Every first draft, redraft, material edit and exact-version review must complete all three analytical passes. They are hats worn by the actual reviewer, not three separate reviewer identities. They do not count as three Hive seats, do not satisfy the five-seat/two-round mailbox gate, and must never be attributed to Grok, Claude, SpaceXSI or another seat unless that person actually performed the work.

### Hat 1 — Evidence and adversarial challenge
- Test every material factual assertion against the original source, not a summary, catalogue entry, app label or another agent's conclusion.
- Keep the strongest contrary record visible. Distinguish VERIFIED, REPORTED BY ANOTHER AGENT, INFERENCE and UNRESOLVED.
- Test scope, parties, dates, place identity, parcel identity, title/record relationship, agency, chronology and any quoted wording.
- Ask what the recipient could fairly say in response and what evidence would falsify the draft's central proposition.
- Do not convert delay, a missing catalogue item, an inadequate answer, a later discovery or a disputed name into an allegation of fraud, concealment, bad faith or deliberate destruction.

### Hat 2 — Recipient, records-custodian and procedure audit
- Identify the exact legal body that received the communication and whether it is subject to the proposed route for this information.
- Determine the correct regime for each distinct information category. Check the current official statute, regulator guidance, recipient policy and actual prior correspondence.
- Reconcile the complete thread: every relevant inbound and sent message, original request, clarification, acknowledgement, extension, substantive response, internal review, complaint, withdrawal, delivery failure and attachment.
- Capture reference numbers, receipt dates, clock start/stop events, statutory deadlines, notified extensions, recipient-promised dates, review/appeal route and any fee or clarification process separately.
- Check whether the addressee is the correct custodian and whether another department, predecessor authority, archive, contractor or successor body is indicated by the recipient's own records.

### Hat 3 — Editorial, legal-precision and CSSCPSG pass
- Make the message proportionate, courteous, concise and specific. Do not recite the recipient's own email back to them unless a short quotation is necessary to identify a precise discrepancy.
- Each question must request an identifiable recorded item, decision, search detail or clarification that is within the recipient's remit. Do not ask the recipient to create a record, reconstruct an opinion, decide title or determine a legal dispute unless the recipient has expressly offered that service.
- Apply CSSCPSG sentence by sentence: actor, act, object and scope must be clear. Check S1 scope, S2 party, S3 agency, S4 term shift, S5 text gap and S6 inconsistency. Record each material mark and the reason.
- Remove unsupported legal conclusions, rhetorical questions, repeated requests, speculative motive, needless CCs, irrelevant PSs and stale or corrected aliases.
- Preserve the exact current owner sign-off required by JOIN.md.

## 2. Fourth-excuse sweep — the strongest fair alternative

After the three hats, perform a separate fourth-excuse sweep. For every apparent gap, refusal, delay, transfer or inconsistent answer, ask what the strongest ordinary, lawful or technical explanation might be. Test, where relevant:

1. The requested record was never created, or the activity did not generate a record.
2. It exists but is held by a different service, legal entity, archive, contractor, predecessor or successor authority.
3. It is indexed under a different date, name, identifier, spelling, series, map sheet, property, parcel or system.
4. The search was limited by the request's wording, dates, location, custodian, repository, search terms or available legacy systems.
5. The item is publicly available, already supplied, outside scope, duplicated, or requires a different access route.
6. A clarification, valid clock pause, lawful extension, transfer, fee procedure, cost limit, exemption, EIR exception, confidentiality restriction or third-party consultation affects the response.
7. The requested record is not held now, but may have been transferred, returned, destroyed, disposed of, lost, misfiled or not located; these are different propositions and must not be collapsed.
8. An archive or records service distinguishes statutory disclosure from supervised access, copying, digitisation, reproduction rights, research services or chargeable work.
9. A later discovery reflects a new search, new index, newly supplied identifier, transfer, digitisation or different scope rather than proving that an earlier officer deliberately withheld it.

The sweep is not an excuse to accept an inadequate answer. It is a discipline for asking the next precise question without overstating what the evidence proves. Where a reasonable alternative remains open, use neutral wording and ask for the specific search, record, transfer or decision evidence that would distinguish the alternatives.

## 3. Thread memory and recipient dialogue are mandatory drafting inputs

Before drafting or redrafting in HubSpot:
- Read the whole relevant live Gmail thread in chronological order, including the owner's earlier sent messages and the recipient's replies. Search for related earlier correspondence with that same recipient where it affects the same subject, request, undertaking, deadline or process.
- Inspect all accessible attachments that materially affect the question. Record filename, sender, date, reference and what was actually inspected. If an attachment is missing, inaccessible, unreadable or not yet examined, say so and keep any dependent question or conclusion unresolved.
- Extract a concise private chronology: what was asked; what the recipient acknowledged; what it answered; what it refused or said was not held; the searches and repositories it disclosed; corrections or clarifications it made; its policy/review route; all commitments and deadlines; what has since been resolved or superseded.
- Distinguish an actual sent Gmail message from a HubSpot proposal, Gmail draft, unsent attachment, quoted text or another agent's summary. A draft is not sent; a forwarded file is not proof of receipt; an acknowledgement is not a substantive response.
- Do not repeat a question already answered, withdrawn, superseded or assigned to another open request. Do not silently widen a request or merge separate references. Preserve separate FOIA/EIR/SAR, complaint, preservation and archive-access lanes.
- Draft as the next turn in the existing relationship, not as a fresh first approach. Recognise useful assistance and earlier commitments where appropriate; correct the record briefly where needed; do not imply an apology, concession, agreement or undertaking that was not made.
- Decide and record whether the correct action is no email, a reply in the existing thread, a new request, a clarification, an internal review, a complaint, an archive enquiry or a hold/withdrawal decision. Explain why a further message is necessary and what new result it is intended to obtain.

If the complete thread or a material attachment cannot be checked, do not edit recipient-facing wording, count a review or advance a mailbox-release gate. Record the precise inaccessible item in HubSpot and route or wait under the live turn rules.

## 4. Dartboard and That Old Chestnut are navigation tools, not primary proof

Use the current Dartboard and That Old Chestnut applications to discover:
- the recipient's correspondence lane and request history;
- earlier response wording, dates, references, deadlines and review outcomes;
- cases where a first 'not held', refusal or out-of-scope response was later followed by records being found;
- unanswered questions, linked routes, possible duplicate requests and the next useful record.

Then open the underlying source: original Gmail message/thread, original attachment, official publication, statutory instrument, policy or catalogue record. The webapps are derived research/navigation views. Their labels, counters, clocks, colour, chronology or summary do not by themselves prove what a recipient received, what it searched, whether a statutory deadline ran, what law applies, or whether an allegation is true. Verify the data item and latest version against the source. Record the app version/date checked and any mismatch privately in HubSpot.

For an apparent 'not held' then 'found' sequence, compare the exact original request scope, original search description, later search terms and custodians, discovery date, document provenance and whether the later item falls within the original scope. Record whether the later reply corrects, supplements or merely changes the earlier position. Do not infer intentional concealment from the sequence alone.

## 5. Regime classification — never use 'FOI' as a universal label

Before writing the subject or opening paragraph, classify every requested information group and verify the recipient's legal coverage and the correct route. Possible routes include:
- Freedom of Information Act 2000 (FOIA);
- Environmental Information Regulations 2004 (EIR);
- UK GDPR / Data Protection Act 2018 subject access or other data-protection rights;
- court, tribunal or prosecution-service records access rules;
- archive, library, heritage or collection-access procedures;
- local-government access-to-meetings and committee-paper rules;
- ordinary customer-service, mapping, register, property or administrative enquiries;
- complaint, internal review, regulator appeal or preservation correspondence.

Do not assume that a public body, a land-related document, a map, a planning record or an environmental organisation makes every requested record an EIR request. Apply the statutory definition to the information sought and the recipient's current official position. One email may contain distinct information groups that the recipient properly logs under different regimes or references; preserve those divisions. Conversely, do not split a single request merely to manufacture routes or clocks.

Use the following as starting points only; verify applicability and current guidance for the actual request:
- FOIA s10: normally prompt response within 20 working days. Any additional time to consider the public-interest test is not automatic; test the statutory provisions, current ICO guidance and the recipient's explanation. The s45 Code of Practice is guidance on desirable practice, including internal reviews; do not describe a FOIA internal review as a statutory EIR-style right.
- EIR reg 5(2): normally as soon as possible and within 20 working days. Reg 7 permits extension to 40 working days only where complexity and volume make compliance impracticable, with notice no later than the initial 20 working days. Reg 11 provides the statutory reconsideration route and a maximum 40-working-day decision period.
- SAR: the usual response period is one month; a further period of up to two months may apply where the statutory conditions for complexity or multiple requests are met, with notice and reasons within the first month. A SAR concerns personal data; do not use it as a substitute for access to non-personal estate or company records.
- The clock must be calculated from the correct receipt date and applicable rules. A mere acknowledgement or holding letter does not necessarily satisfy the substantive response duty. Clarification, transfer, identity checks, fee requests, non-working days and extensions must be analysed under the applicable route, not assumed.
- A recipient's published service standard, an internal target date, a date it volunteered, a statutory deadline and a review target are separate. Record each separately and never silently substitute one for another.

Official starting points (re-check for updates at each relevant review):
- FOIA: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/36/contents
- EIR: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2004/3391/contents
- ICO FOIA timing: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/freedom-of-information-and-environmental-information-regulations/time-limits-for-compliance-under-the-freedom-of-information-act-section-10/
- ICO EIR timing: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/freedom-of-information-and-environmental-information-regulations/time-limits-for-compliance-under-the-eir/
- ICO request handling and internal reviews: https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/foi/freedom-of-information-and-environmental-information-regulations/request-handling-freedom-of-information/

## 6. Outcome vocabulary and evidential discipline

Classify each request point separately. Use precise dispositions:
- disclosed in full;
- partly disclosed;
- withheld/refused under a cited provision;
- not held (as at the response date and within the described search);
- not located after the disclosed search;
- never created (only where the recipient actually says this);
- transferred or held by another identified body;
- destroyed/disposed of (only where supported by a record or explicit response);
- publicly available / already supplied;
- outside scope / requires clarification;
- fee or cost process invoked;
- acknowledgement or holding response only;
- no response by a calculated deadline;
- review/complaint outcome;
- later-discovered record requiring comparison with the earlier search.

Do not use 'destroyed', 'lost', 'concealed', 'missing at transfer', 'not held' or 'not created' as interchangeable labels. An absence of a record of transfer is not proof that no transfer occurred. A catalogue statement is not the underlying instrument. A recipient's own explanation is a reported institutional position until independently corroborated. Keep contrary evidence and limitations.

## 7. Preservation language and allegation control

Do not state that FOIA s77 creates a general preservation duty or that every open request automatically imposes a case-specific legal hold. Section 77 concerns specified acts against records with the intention of preventing disclosure after a qualifying request. The s46 records-management Code is a records-management framework; it is not, without more, proof of a blanket legal hold in an individual case.

A preservation request should identify the records, the relevant open matters, the requested duration and the action sought. Ask the recipient to confirm whether a hold or other preservation measure has actually been applied, who/which function owns it, and the relevant retention/disposal policy or recorded decision. Distinguish a request for preservation from a statutory FOI/EIR request, complaint or SAR. Any claim of unlawful destruction, concealment, fraud, bad faith or deliberate obstruction must be held as an allegation/hypothesis unless evidence supports each element; do not put such conclusions into a routine draft.

## 8. Private recipient profile — required in HubSpot, never in public GitHub

Every active recipient draft must have a current, source-linked private profile in its HubSpot task (or a linked private HubSpot control record). Populate only what has been verified. Do not copy profile contents, contact details, case identifiers, correspondence or draft wording into GitHub, public Dartboard metadata or Hive chat.

Required profile fields:
1. Exact legal entity, relevant department/service and whether the relevant function is within the statutory scheme.
2. Regime by information category, with a short reason; do not assume one regime applies to all categories.
3. Current official request guidance, publication scheme, FOI/EIR/SAR policy, archive/reproduction/access policy and complaints/review procedure.
4. Submission channel, required form/reference details, how the recipient asks requests to be identified, and any published limits or exclusions.
5. Response clock, calculation rule, extension conditions, notification rule and recipient-promised date, recorded separately.
6. Internal review/reconsideration and external regulator/appeal route, eligibility, time limit and published target.
7. Fees, cost limits, copying/digitisation charges, exemptions/exceptions and any need for advance approval.
8. Known repositories, custodians, predecessor/successor bodies, transfer route and retention/disposal policy relevant to this request.
9. Chronology of this recipient's actual prior dialogue: promises, acknowledgements, clarifications, corrections, searches disclosed, answers, refusals, changed positions and still-open questions.
10. Recipient-specific wording constraints, corrected identifiers/names, no-repeat items, attachment requirements and owner-only decisions.
11. Official source URL(s), date checked, reviewer, what was verified and what remains unresolved.

Profiles are recipient- and route-specific. A policy for one council, archive, agency or police force must not be copied to another without checking. Update the profile after each substantive response, policy change, internal review or new source. Never treat a profile's last-checked date as proof that a later policy remains current.
